# FATE · Señales del cliente — estados, no veredictos

Diagnostic lens inspired by the verbal-indicator and baselining frameworks in
Chase Hughes' *The Behavior Ops Manual* (the text-transferable subset),
applied to READ CUSTOMER STATE so the business can serve better. Original
articulation; not affiliated with or endorsed by the author.

HARD RULES for this dimension — they outrank everything else:
- You read STATES (uncertainty, friction, disengagement), never intent. You
  NEVER label an individual customer as lying, deceptive or manipulative.
- No single cue means anything. Only clusters of 2+ signals tied to the same
  moment count, and every flag must list its benign explanations first
  (mobile typing, off-hours, personal style, the business's own prior message
  causing the reaction).
- If the business's preceding message was pushy or accusatory, discount the
  customer's stress signals — the trigger contaminates the read — and report
  the business's message as the finding instead.
- Findings are aggregate patterns across threads, never a dossier on one
  person.

Examine, with counted evidence:

1. **Reassurance deficits**: inbound uncertainty markers (repeated questions
   about the same detail, "¿es seguro?", "no estoy seguro", validation tags
   like "¿sí?", "¿verdad?") — and whether the reply answered with concrete
   risk-reducers (guarantee, return policy, delivery specifics) or ignored
   the discomfort.
2. **Opinion-suppression collapse**: engagement collapse right after a
   proposal (long replies dropping to "ok", "dale", single emoji) as a
   withheld objection. Count conversations lost after such a collapse point
   and whether the business ever invited the unspoken concern.
3. **Masked friction**: politely-worded inbound messages carrying friction
   markers (sarcastic thanks, clipped formality shifts, "smh"-type tokens)
   that precede complaints or churn. Aggregate the pattern.
4. **Evasion as unanswered questions**: for each direct question the CLIENT
   asked, was it answered, deflected or ignored by the business? (The
   no-answer metric cuts both ways — here it audits the business.)
5. **Per-contact deviation, not absolutes**: a short reply from a always-brief
   client means nothing; the same reply from a usually-expansive client is a
   state change. Anchor every read to that contact's own baseline within the
   corpus.
6. **Decision-maker mapping** (group-ish threads or relayed decisions): when
   the client defers ("lo consulto con mi socio"), does the business equip the
   real decision-maker (a forwardable summary) or keep pitching the messenger?

Suggested row columns: signal cluster, evidence (threads + quote), benign
explanations considered, how the business responded, recommendation.
