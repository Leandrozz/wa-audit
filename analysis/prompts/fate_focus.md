# FATE · Foco — captura y retención de atención

Diagnostic lens inspired by the FATE model (Focus / Authority / Tribe /
Emotion) and adjacent frameworks in Chase Hughes' *The Behavior Ops Manual*,
applied as an AUDIT of the business's own communication. Original articulation;
not affiliated with or endorsed by the author.

Focus is the gate: nothing else in a conversation works until attention is
captured, and text channels grant only seconds of it. Analyze how well the
business EARNS and HOLDS attention — never how to exploit it.

Examine, with counted evidence:

1. **First-touch attention**: are openers personalized and specific (referencing
   what the client actually asked) or recycled templates? Near-identical
   opening messages across many threads = scripted, low-attention messaging.
   Where both patterns exist, compare their reply rates.
2. **Dead-end replies**: business answers that close the conversation — a bare
   fact ("sale 500") with zero hooks to continue (no option, no question, no
   next step). Count threads that stall immediately after one.
3. **Focus discipline**: does each thread hold one clear topic per message, or
   walls of text and scattered multi-topic dumps that dilute what the client
   retains? Long bursts of fragmented consecutive messages count against.
4. **Loss-vs-reward framing**: classify persuasion framing in outbound messages
   as urgency/scarcity-based ("quedan 2", "el precio sube") vs benefit-based.
   Over-reliance on artificial urgency is BOTH a style finding and an ethics
   flag — report it as such.
5. **Attention recovery**: when a client goes quiet, does the business follow
   up? After how long? With a pattern-breaking message or the same template?

Suggested row columns: attention pattern, evidence (threads + quote), how
often, effect observed, recommendation.
