# FATE · Tribu — pertenencia, espejo y prueba social

Diagnostic lens inspired by the FATE model and the rapport/conformity
frameworks in Chase Hughes' *The Behavior Ops Manual*, applied as an AUDIT of
the business's own communication. Original articulation; not affiliated with
or endorsed by the author.

People trust whoever makes them feel understood and part of the in-group.
In text this is measurable as mirroring, acknowledgment, and honest social
proof. Analyze both directions of the conversation.

Examine, with counted evidence:

1. **Language matching**: does the business reuse the client's own words for
   products, units and problems ("la cinta ancha" vs the catalog's SKU name),
   and paraphrase the request back before answering? Feeling understood is the
   measurable core of charisma; template boilerplate is its opposite.
2. **Acknowledge-then-answer**: when a client shares context, doubt or
   pushback, does the reply validate it first ("entiendo que parezca caro")
   before countering — or jump cold to the rebuttal or the pitch?
3. **Social proof usage**: count belonging/consensus statements ("la mayoría
   de nuestros clientes de zona sur elige...", "es el más pedido"). Are they
   used at objection moments? Are they PLAUSIBLY TRUE given the corpus? Flag
   social proof that the data contradicts — invented consensus is a trust
   time-bomb.
4. **Face-saving exits**: when the client made a mistake or retreats (wrong
   order, missed payment, backing out), does the reply offer a graceful path
   ("le pasa a cualquiera") or blame? Count golden-bridge vs told-you-so
   responses.
5. **Collaborative vs competitive framing**: "lo resolvemos juntos", "veamos
   qué te conviene" versus arguing to win the point, "actually...", sarcasm.
   Ratio per conversation.
6. **Memory of the tribe member**: does the business reference what THIS client
   said earlier in the thread or in past orders — or re-ask for information
   already given and treat repeat clients as strangers?

Suggested row columns: tribe signal, evidence (threads + quote), frequency,
trust effect, recommendation.
